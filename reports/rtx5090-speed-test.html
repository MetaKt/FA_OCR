What it means for the webapp

  • 40-page batch
    The webapp's stated maximum batch. About 4 minutes on the RTX 5090, inside the 10-minute limit for one batch, one page at a time. The laptop would need about 25 minutes, so it cannot handle a batch that size.
  • 300-page job
    About 30 minutes on the RTX 5090, against roughly 3 hours on the laptop.
  • Headroom
    The RTX 5090 used 10 of its 32 GB. That spare memory is what would let it work on several batches at once, but that was not tested (see below).

What this test does not show

  • Accuracy. This test only timed the machines. It did not check the answers against the answer key. The same models and settings were used, so the answers should be very close to the laptop's, but different graphics cards can round numbers slightly differently. That has not been checked on the RTX 5090.
  • Several users at once. Pages went through one at a time. How the RTX 5090 performs with several batches arriving together is still unmeasured, and it is what sizing a shared server depends on.
  • Other document types. All 15 pages were scanned cash bills. Toll tickets and long multi-page receipts were not in this set, and no page needed the slower second read that the pipeline does on hard pages.
  • Where the documents would live. The test ran on a rented cloud machine using test documents only. The company prefers to keep real documents on its own hardware, so these numbers describe what an RTX 5090-class card delivers, not a proposal to process real documents in the cloud.

How it was measured. Both machines ran the same benchmark in the project's pipeline notebook: two scanned cash-bill documents (5 and 10 pages), stage 1 typhoon-ocr1.5-3b and stage 2 qwen3:4b, identical settings and pipeline code. Both models were loaded and run once before timing started, and that warm-up is not counted. Times are the average over all 15 pages. The server adds a few checks on top of these two stages; they were measured earlier at about 0.1% of the time and are left out. Both runs were on 18 September 2026; the rental was a Vast.ai instance.
